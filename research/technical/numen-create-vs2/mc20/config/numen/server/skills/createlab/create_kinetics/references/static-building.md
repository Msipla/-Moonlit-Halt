# Two-storey workshop and home

1. Read `engineering.site.info()` once for the authorized corners, ground height, batch size and available materials. Survey a few specific columns only if they can change the site or the entrance. Plan the footprint and outline, storey levels, palette and phase budgets. Estimate usable area from the room dimensions. Estimate construction from the floors, perimeter, roof and interior rather than filling the volume.

   On resumption, use the confirmed building footprint and floor heights. The authorized site's outer corners may be empty, and `center_surface` may be the upper floor. Check just the shared boundary or the candidate in `engineering.site.inspect_many` (up to 16 positions). Then submit the next usable section in the same stage and report the actual receipt before starting another section.
2. Give the ground floor a workshop and a route from the exterior door to every destination. Reserve the stair run and its upper landing before writing the upper floor. Leave a clear bottom landing aligned with the first tread, with room to turn before climbing. Give the rooms, kitchen, storage and balcony access from that route. Put use faces of furniture and machine inputs and outputs next to standing positions. Keep doors, railings and beams clear of the stair and its approaches.
3. Generate sections with Lua loops or `shape` and keep the final state for each position once in each submitted section. Build floors and structural supports before the details they support. For materials in `engineering.site.info().blocks`, call `my.create_survey.construct(cells)` with `{pos={x=x,y=y,z=z},state="id[properties]"}`. It splits at 64 and checks and reads back each batch. Save the confirmed range and inspect any uncertain range before resuming. Read live state before requesting deliberate replacement. Use the next section only after its shared boundary is usable.
4. In a bounded, authorized build, use native `numen.build.place` for a small section of architectural blocks absent from the site palette. It takes `{pos={x=x,y=y,z=z},name="id[properties]"}`. Place supports first, use the lower door half or bed foot as the placement target, and read both parts afterwards. Inspect stair direction and landing clearance, lantern attachment and connections of rails. Limit native submissions to 64 intended positions and account for automatic second halves and any scaffolding in the readback.
5. Verify the foot and head path in short sections with `engineering.site.plan({walk=...})` where the planner supports the geometry. For stairs, independently verify direction, headroom directly over each tread, the opening in the upper floor and actual survival movement in both directions. For a stepped roof, view the cross-section from the gable and follow the upper surface from eave to ridge to catch dips between stairs and full blocks. Compare windows and eaves on more than one façade. Use a trim or change of depth to distinguish the entrance and balcony from plain walls. Light the landings and work surfaces as well as the rooms.
6. Keep the top of a driven millstone accessible to the intended input. Read actual `engineering.site.inspect(load)` RPM and leave the input and output available for the owner's separate first and repeat trials. Check any replaced container still has its previous stock. After each building stage, return the usable areas, actual placed or confirmed count, connecting points, remaining budget and the next unfinished section. Report unresolved areas in the final reply.
